# Bold Design Moves — KK's page

**How to look at the page.** The page is covered by an "Epilepsy Warning" modal (the button "I am ready" / "Back to safe space"), and behind it is a rickroll (a GIF over the heart and the sound `audio/rickroll.mp3`). This is part of the page's character: provocation and humour that test at once whether the reader is "one of us". Almost all the meaning lives in the images (SVGs with handwritten lettering and PNG previews of real work); the text in the HTML is only captions. Below is a walk through the sections from top to bottom, with a description of every image.

**Gist.** Design is not turning out mock-ups but searching for **moves**: solutions that multiply the business result. A move almost always starts by reframing the request: the client brings a solution ("we need drag-n-drop", "three tabs", "a meme with Confucius"), and the designer finds the real problem and a simpler, stronger solution. For a move to be possible, four layers of work are needed: strategy (a 10× goal, not low-hanging fruit), tactics (repair the process that delivers solutions), execution (flawless craft + supervision + 15 competencies) and transformation (a system, standards, a team, so that quality becomes predictable).

## Principles and rules
- **The unit of value is a move, not an artifact.** A move is a solution that changes the result (money, speed, activation, trust), often by giving up the requested solution.
- **The client's request is a hypothesis about the solution, not the task.** Every preview is built as "they said → we saw": behind "we need drag-n-drop" stands "the user needs to edit the article's structure", behind "three tabs" stands "the user needs the whole picture at once", behind "make it look good" stands "users are drowning in tables".
- **The goal is 10×, not +10%.** "I don't care about low hanging fruits. We'll have a strategy to grab the whole tree." The path to 10× is a product that is fast, clear and rich with insights; then marketing, sales and support become simpler too.
- **First remove false projections.** Interviews, product mapping, usage statistics: before the vision.
- **Calibrate the vision with experiments before investing.** Usability sessions record where the user loses trust, does not understand, is overwhelmed, does not read.
- **Tactics means repairing the system that delivers solutions.** Aligning the inputs, simple scoring, shaping before the kick-off, standardized solving and testing, a feedback loop. "Don't panic. We'll start here."
- **Compare the result with the current product, not with an ideal** (the baseline from Shape Up): "Don't stick to idealistic approach where you try to deliver the best design ever". This does not contradict "the best solution": the best one is among those actually achievable now, given the appetite.
- **The craft is not up for discussion.** All scenarios, priorities, the right visualization, user habits, scalability, cross-platform use, and iterations "till the panic attack".
- **Supervision keeps the vision sharp and honest.** An outside view, a network of experts.
- **Fifteen competencies.** Strategy (goal, vision, principles), culture (team, processes, standards), research (competition, interviews, statistics), design (drafts, solutions, prototypes), feedback (1-1s, retro, agreements).
- **Transformation = predictability.** A design system, standards, team training, so that product and marketing "signal value in a clear and consistent way".
- **A number without context is noise.** In all of KK's interfaces a number comes with a delta, a trend, a comparison, a range or a verbal rating (Easy/Doable/Hard); a metric is explained by breaking it down into its causes.

## How the author reasons
The page itself is built as a process and as a metaphor: a whole apple (strategy: "the whole tree") → a bite (execution) → the core (transformation and results) → a leaf (what comes next). Every block is a one-line claim (highlighted in pink at the start of the sentence) + proof by real work + a caption with the mechanism ("to…, I do…"). Nowhere is there an abstract "we value quality": only specific moves with numbers.

## Gotchas
- Do not carry out the request literally: the requested solution is often a dead end (drag-n-drop instead of a text editor would have cost ~$30K).
- Do not start with a vision without research: "false projections" are the main reason money gets wasted.
- Do not drag everything into the backlog, and do not inflate impact scores with UI trifles.
- Do not chase "the best design ever" at the cost of deadlines: compare with the current product.
- Do not show bare numbers: without a delta, a comparison and an explanation the user loses trust (the case of "equal numbers in Total and additional traffic").
- Do not hide data across tabs when the user's task is to see the whole picture.

## How to apply
- Before a solution: which "they said" did I get, and which "we saw" stands behind it? State the move in one sentence.
- Which result are we multiplying (activation, trust, time, money)? What does the 10× version look like?
- What do I know from data, and what is a projection? Where do I get interviews, statistics, behaviour?
- What minimal experiment will test the vision before the investment?
- What is getting in the way of the delivery system (inputs, priorities, acceptance criteria)?
- Check the craft: all scenarios, the right visualization, habits, scale, platforms.
- Compare the result with the current state, not with an imagined ideal.
