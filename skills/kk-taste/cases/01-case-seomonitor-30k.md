# $30K saved in three calls at niche SEO tool

**Question/task.** "Revising the product direction to avoid a clear dead end. In a rush." "SEOmonitor is an analytics platform for 2000 UK & US agencies. Each of them tracks tens of thousands keywords. In 2023, the team focused on the new product for AI Content Writing. Team needed to deliver it fast."

**Gist.** A dead-end solution, handed down from the top in a rush, was stopped not by an argument about taste but by three conversations: signalling the problem → reframing the problem in terms of money and risk → choosing together between options on wireframes. The simple solution won. It rests on a model the user already knows (a text editor), and it still works months later.

## Principles and rules
- **Signalling a problem is a duty, even if "there is no room for discussion."**
- **Prepare the counter-proposal before the conversation:** an audit of the direction plus a sketch of a simpler option. Criticism without an alternative does not work.
- **If the signal is not heard, reframe it in the language of the person who decides:** not "this is inconvenient" but "the rush will burn 30K euros, and then we will have to redo it."
- **Propose a change to the process, not only to the solution:** the designer takes part in the direction discussion from day one.
- **The decision is made together, on wireframes, with explicit pros and cons**, following the sequence "problem → initial solution → options → bet".
- **Simple solution = a familiar mental model + existing components.** A text editor is familiar from Google Docs and Word, there is no modality, there are fewer components, and design, development and testing cost less.
- **Distinguish states by colour:** the outline and the final article use different colour schemes, so the user does not confuse the draft with the result.

## Gotchas
- Do not stay silent when you see a dead end: "there is no room for discussion" is not an excuse.
- Do not repeat the same argument to the same person: repetition without reframing = noise.
- Do not choose a solution that requires a new mental model from the user when a familiar one exists.
- Do not start hi-fi before there is agreement on the vision: aligning on wireframes is cheaper.

## How to apply
- If the task arrives as a ready-made solution, audit it: what will be hard to design, implement, use and explain?
- Sketch a simpler option on familiar patterns and existing components.
- Calculate the cost of a mistake (money, weeks, rework): this is the language decisions are made in.
- Lay the options side by side, write down the pros, cons and critical risks, then choose and explain the bet.
