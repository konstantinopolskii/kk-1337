# $1M saved in costs with design execution

**Question/task.** "Changing the team direction at a 100M users social network." "In 2023 Bandlab's Social Network department was focused on three product directions: music exploration, newsfeed, and chat." KK joined the Chat team as a Senior Product Designer.

**Gist.** The team was burning money trying to meet its OKR, "grow the usage by 5 times", relying on statistics and examples from the world rather than on a validated vision. A newcomer to the team took the lead over validating the vision: documented the research, got the budget, ran the interviews, designed **the best vision possible** and let users **vote** for it (KANO). It turned out that users did not need chat improvements at all, and the team pivoted to collaboration on music (the Bands concept). About $1M was saved.

## Principles and rules
- **A vision without validation is a blind bet.** If any mistake costs weeks, validation is cheaper.
- **Frame the initiative as a document:** goals, team, method, sample, duration, budget, guide. That is how you get the budget and the agreement.
- **Interviews show segments and patterns, but not priorities.** When it seems that "they don't need anything", do not stop: make the best vision possible and let people choose.
- **KANO separates "Must Have" from "Nice-to-Have" and "Don't-Do-It".** Illustrate the survey so that people rate the same thing; the survey itself also goes through a UX test.
- **A negative result is still a result.** "Nobody needs it" is worth a million if you learn it before development.
- **A research result must become a business bet:** insight → pivot proposal → management decision.
- **A newcomer can change the direction too**, through data and alignment with the manager.

## Gotchas
- Do not pursue an OKR head-on when the strategy is not validated: 5× growth will not come from improvements nobody needs.
- Do not trust only statistics and "how others do it": statistics show what is happening, not why.
- Do not end the research at "they don't need anything": it means the question was asked the wrong way.
- Do not run a survey without illustrations: without them, each person rates something different.

## How to apply
- Before a large amount of work, ask: what confirms that this is needed? How much does a mistake cost?
- Formulate the best version of the vision and find the cheapest way for users to speak up about each part of it (KANO, a prototype, a fake door).
- Sort the features into must-haves, desirable, indifferent and harmful; build the must-haves.
- Translate the research conclusion into a business decision: what we change, how much we save, what we risk.
