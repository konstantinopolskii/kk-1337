# KK's philosophy: models in your head and handing over a task without losing the signal

**Question/task.** How to reach the level of strategy, and how to run projects so that the result matches what is really needed. The conversation is about career growth, but in it KK states the foundation of his method.

**Gist.** Strategic vision is accumulated models: you have seen many failures, dug yourself out of them, read how others did it, and built a system for deciding which information to trust. That is how taste and intuition appear. But they are imperfect, and signals from reality check them. Work starts with three things: **understand the task** (without skipping questions), **agree on the end result** by giving it back to the client and the team **as a mirror, without losing the signal, in a structured way**, and **sort it by priority**.

## Principles and rules
- **Strategy = leveled-up models.** "The models in your head level up, and you see some steps ahead" («У тебя прокачиваются модели в твоей голове, и ты видишь наперёд некоторые шаги»). Sources of models: a lot of "crap" you have seen; experience of digging out of it; reading how others dug out; your own hierarchy of trust in information.
- **Taste and intuition are imperfect.** An example from a launch: there are few people so far, little data, and the signal does not reach a purchase, so intuition was wrong about how long the system needs to "settle in" («приладки»). Confidence in the direction ≠ a guarantee of hitting the target. The conclusion for work: record an intuitive decision as a hypothesis and check it against data.
- **Understand the task.** The processes for clarifying a task should be second nature (literally "bounce off your teeth", «отскакивать от зубов»): do not skip questions; understand from which side to look at the course of the project; account for risks already while defining the scope; collect from whoever sets the task (or from the team) as much information as possible about what "the project worked out" means.
- **Agree on the end result: the mirroring principle.** Give back the described result and the whole "chunk of data" with no loss of signal: everything the team, the client and the manager talked about must end up in the project documentation; not a single detail gets lost; the reflection is a mirror image, with no data loss, structured, "written out very neatly" («очень чётенько расписано»). Exactly this kind of document synchronizes the team and confirms that everyone understood the same thing.
- **The right breakdown by priority** is the third pillar.
- **Tools and theory of running a project:** trackers, databases, spreadsheets, integration tools; theories: agile, Remote, Shape Up; managing your own time; keeping track of expectations, negotiation; working with target metrics.
- **The lead of an area sees the scope ahead:** knowing how each project is going, they build out the development of the area; what comes to the fore is communication with the team, estimating capacity and resources, flexibility, solving problems "right now", and finding solutions to hard problems.
- **Design rests first of all on text.** That is why the list for leveling up in design includes books on text and typography.
- **The table is the core element.** "If you understand how to design tables, you will most likely understand how to design a lot of things at once" («Если ты поймёшь, как таблицы дизайнить, скорее всего поймёшь, как дизайнить много чего одновременно»). (Compare KK's cases: Rank Tracker, the iBinom report with 40 columns: in each, the key decision was about the table.)

## Sources of models KK recommends
- On product and startups: Lean Startup; Zero to One; Customer Development; Lean UX; Remote (37signals); Shape Up.
- On risk and decisions: Taleb's books (Antifragile, The Black Swan, Skin in the Game); Isaacson's biography of Steve Jobs, "to get a feel for how he made decisions".
- On organizations and yourself: Laloux's "Reinventing Organizations" (teal organizations); GTD; Do It Tomorrow.
- On negotiation: Jim Camp, "Start with No"; Gavin Kennedy, "Everything Is Negotiable".
- On design: Typography for Lawyers (Butterick); The Elements of Style (on text); the gestalt principles from Intuition (intuition.team; see library/gestalt/); Igor Shtang's course on tables.

## How the author reasons
From the career question ("how do I make a plan to reach the goal?") KK moves to how competence is built: there is no simple plan, because you change and the world changes. So instead of a plan there is a list of pillar skills and a way to accumulate them (projects + reading + reflection). For project work he marks the "key points" before the structure: "we went into structure too early; I would first just mark the key points" («мы рано ушли в структуру, я бы сначала просто ключевые точки обозначил»). First the pillars, then the tree.

## Gotchas
- Do not go into structure before the key points are marked.
- Do not lose details said by the client or the team when you carry them into the documentation: loss of signal = a team out of sync.
- Do not take intuition's confidence for proof: check it against data.

## How to apply (for Claude)
- At the start of a task: gather everything the user said and showed and **mirror back** your understanding (what we are making, for whom, what counts as success, the constraints), losing nothing and adding nothing of your own without marking it.
- Mark where a decision rests on a model or intuition and where it rests on data; check the intuitive ones (a render, a test, a comparison).
- Try any dense information as a table first: a table disciplines the structure.
- Text comes first: wording first, then layout.
