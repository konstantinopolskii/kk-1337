# KK's bar for the look: identify the soul, use the contrasts

**Question/task.** The first evals of kk-design produced pages that were right on structure and wrong on look: KK found them "a little bit less western and less ipad/touch device era… too much bureau". He then set the bar for the look and sent the references that meet it.

**Gist.** The look is not a style to copy but a method: "we need to identify the soul and use the contrasts". Name what the product is like as a person, carry it with a few strong contrasts, and give it one element that is truly beautiful and unique. Even the typeface follows the soul: "grotesk or not grotesk depends on the style". His own Jazzylea posters set a huge serif against a bold grotesk, because the soul there is an elegant jazz evening. Out: the Linear look, print-editorial pages on products, and the average of galleries.

## KK's words
- "Bold, clean, swiss and stylish thoughtful design with parts that are truly beautiful and unique."
- On sources: the "best" design "will also show you a lot of garbage if you will try to look in mobbin or google. So we need someting minimalistic to inpsire from. Something like Aleksei Sekachev, Apple Design Awards for interfaces."
- On what to avoid: "Not Linear-like". Asked what that means, he confirmed: dark glowing gradients, glass cards, bento grids, tiny grey type, the same dark SaaS look everywhere.
- On the method: "grotesk or not grotesk depends on the style. So we need to identify the soul and use the contrasts."
- On DRIVE2's parts page: "a funny example of soul interface when we have a real boxes right in the interface, with 3d, I love such playful ideas".
- On the form of examples: "the html mockups are good as example".
- On standards: he wants "very hard standards to what we will take as reference".

Other screens KK sent share the same traits: a tractor dispatch schedule (status tints keep a dense day calm; one loud yellow action with a count), an invoice generator (the document is the interface; editable fields tinted, a cartoon face), a phone CRM (a large title, a bottom sheet, a full-width yellow button), a backup service ("Pipedrive" huge, everything else grey data), an ICE table ("Ideas 9", the deciding score as the loudest number), a notes app with a toast "You are fucking up the deadline" in the product's own voice, and two designers' portfolios made of statements.

## What the references share
- **One huge hero per screen:** a name, a number or a statement, several times the body size; the rest small and quiet.
- **The data is the design:** numbers as heroes with their change beside them, statements instead of labels.
- **Black, white and a colour with a job,** in flat areas: a button, a header, a status tint. A gradient only when it is the soul's own light (Jazzylea's stage light), never for mood.
- **Big, confident controls of the touch era:** full-width buttons with soft corners, chips, segmented controls, sheets, large titles; on a phone the content sits low, within reach of the thumb; boxes only where they say "tap here".
- **Bare structure:** tables without boxes, a grid, a lot of air.
- **One unique, human element that carries the soul,** often playful.
